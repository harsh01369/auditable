Why this is urgent, and why it is unusually easy to get wrong

The European Accessibility Act has been enforceable since 28 June 2025. In Germany, e-commerce operators are already receiving warning letters from law firms, because competition law there lets private parties act on non-compliance. In France, injunctions were filed in November 2025 against Auchan, Carrefour, E.Leclerc and Picard.

Penalties reach €100,000, and authorities can also require an audit, ban a non-compliant service, or name the business publicly.

In April 2025 the FTC ordered accessiBe to pay $1,000,000 for claiming its widget could make any website WCAG compliant. Courts have consistently rejected overlay defences, and in the first half of 2025, 22.6% of US web accessibility lawsuits targeted sites that had an overlay installed.

An overlay is not a defence. On the evidence, it is a liability with a subscription attached.

The number nobody selling you a scanner will show you

4 of 55
WCAG 2.2 Level A and AA criteria that a rule engine can fully decide on its own

Another 29 are only partly automatable. 22 cannot be assessed by any automated method at all.

A scanner can tell you an image has no alt attribute. It cannot tell you that the alt text reads "image", that a link labelled "read more" fails 2.4.4, or that a heading does not describe the section beneath it. That judgement is most of an audit, which is why a clean scan is not conformance and why a green dashboard is not a defence.

How we do it

Pass one

Rule engine

axe-core settles what can be decided mechanically. Reproducible: anyone can re-run it and get the same answer.

Pass two

Judgement

A model reads the rendered DOM, the browser-computed accessibility tree and every interactive element, and reports what the engine structurally cannot see.

Pass three

Verification

Every claim is checked against the captured page before it is allowed to be called a finding. Anything unsubstantiated is discarded and listed as rejected.

What verification actually means

A claim reaches your report only if it survives all five checks:

  • the success criterion it cites exists in WCAG 2.2 A/AA;
  • its selector resolves to exactly one element we really captured;
  • the markup it quotes genuinely appears in that element;
  • it does not overturn a criterion the rule engine fully decided and found clean;
  • it does not restate a defect the rule engine already reported.

The model is allowed to be wrong. It is not allowed to be believed. That distinction is the entire difference between this and the product a regulator fined a million dollars.

What we refuse to do

We will not call absence of findings a pass

Every one of the 55 criteria is reported as failed, passed, not established, or needing a person. Only criteria a rule engine fully decides and found clean are reported as passing. On our published sample that is 1 criterion out of 55, and the report says so on the first page.

We will not audit the wrong page

Bot challenges, cookie walls, login screens and error pages all return HTTP 200 and all look clean to a scanner. Our tooling refuses to audit any page it cannot show is the real page. We found this the hard way, auditing a Cloudflare interstitial and nearly believing the result.

We also publish the claims we rejected, inside your report. A report that hides its rejects is indistinguishable from one that never made any, and you deserve to judge the method rather than trust it.
