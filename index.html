      Why the rule engine only found some of it

      What you just ran is a rule engine. It is genuinely good, and it is the first of three passes. But of the 55 success criteria the European Accessibility Act requires, here is how much of the job a rule engine can do alone. Each square is one criterion.

      Decided mechanically, 4
      Partly, 29
      Not at all, 22

      Point at a square. Each one is a success criterion you are legally required to meet.

      The difference, on one image

      Take the third product on that page. Its markup is <img alt="photo">.

      What a scanner concludes

      The image has an alt attribute. It is not empty. The rule is satisfied, and the scan moves on to the next element.

      1.1.1  pass

      What is actually true

      A customer who cannot see the screen is told the word “photo”. They do not learn that it is a 750ml trail flask, or that it costs £28. They cannot decide whether to buy it.

      1.1.1  fail, alternative text names the medium, not the content

      That judgement is most of an accessibility audit, and it is why a clean scan is not conformance. Our second pass is a language model reading the rendered page and the accessibility tree, looking for precisely this.

      And why you can believe the second pass

      Language models invent things. Telling a business it is breaking a law it is not breaking is worse than saying nothing. So no claim from the model reaches your report on the model’s authority. Each one has to survive five checks against your own captured page.

      • The success criterion it cites exists in WCAG 2.2 A or AA.
      • Its selector resolves to exactly one element we really captured.
      • The markup it quotes genuinely appears in that element.
      • It does not overturn a criterion the rule engine fully decided and found clean.
      • It does not restate a defect the rule engine already reported.

      Anything that fails is discarded and printed in your report as rejected, so you can see what it got wrong. A report that hides its mistakes is indistinguishable from one that never made any.

      Measured on a corpus written after the system was tuned, and run once: it found 5 of 7 defects the rule engine structurally cannot catch, with zero false positives on six deliberately correct elements. On the corpus we tuned against it scores 88%. We quote the 71%, because a score from the pages you tuned on is a score you gave yourself. The method and the known weaknesses are published.
